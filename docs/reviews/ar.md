# Arabic — translation review

Standard (user decision 2026-11-12): every string is compared with a similar app or feature in
the product's own Arabic UI, for idiomatic wording only; more popular products rank higher. A
non-gender-neutral or culturally insensitive term is never accepted, even when a compared
product uses it: the product's wording stays here as evidence and the catalog uses a neutral
alternative. The user reviews the Arabic wording where they can; the mechanical checks (parity,
access keys) are the standing gate.

## Products compared (popularity, 1 = most popular)

1. Zoom — Arabic UI: meeting, recording and transcript terms.
2. Microsoft Teams — Arabic UI: meeting transcript and connection terms.
3. Google Workspace (Docs/Drive) — Arabic UI: upload, search, settings.
4. Microsoft Word — Arabic UI: dictation and transcription.
5. Apple (macOS/iOS) — Arabic UI: playback, editing, settings.
6. Otter.ai — Arabic UI: transcription and speakers.
7. Descript — Arabic UI: transcript editing, segments.
8. Amberscript — Arabic UI: transcription service.
9. GNU nano — Arabic gettext catalog: terminal key labels and status-line prompts.
10. GNOME Files (Nautilus) — Arabic UI: file browser, folders, files.

9 and 10 are the TUI's nearest established counterparts for terminal key hints and the
file-browser dialogs; they are ranked after the desktop and transcription products, which
cover the rest of the catalog.

Language: Modern Standard Arabic, the register all ten products use; no dialect.
Address: nominal/masdar style (`التحقق…`, `إلغاء`, `الإفلات…`) rather than the masculine
imperative, so no gender is assumed of the reader. The products' masculine imperatives are
evidence only.
Gender: the products' speaker term `المتحدثون` is masculine; the catalog uses `الأصوات` (voices)
and `المداخلة` (a turn) instead, because the app handles voices, not people. The products'
wording is recorded, never used.
Typography: right-to-left; Arabic question mark `؟`, comma `،`, semicolon `؛`, percent sign
`٪`, `«»` quotes; numbers and dates come from `Intl` (`ar-EG`), in Arabic-Indic digits.
The layout mirroring is the separate RTL item; this catalog is reviewed before it.

"Compared" gives the product and its Arabic wording; the Arabic column is this catalog's text.
