# Hebrew (he) — translation review

Standard (user decision 2026-11-12): every string is compared with a similar app or feature in
the product's own Hebrew UI; more popular products rank higher; every string is audited for
gender neutrality and cultural sensitivity. The mechanical parity check (keys, placeholders,
ICU, plural categories) is the standing gate. The user approved the translations on 2026-11-13;
the review record and the mechanical checks remain the gate.

## Products compared (popularity, 1 = most popular)

1. Google Workspace (Docs/Drive/Meet) — Hebrew UI: upload, search, transcripts, voice typing.
2. Gemini — Hebrew UI: transcription, speakers, summaries.
3. Microsoft (Teams/Word) — Hebrew UI: meeting transcripts, dictation, upload.
4. Apple (macOS/iOS) — Hebrew UI: dictation, playback, editing.
5. Zoom — Hebrew UI: meeting/recording/transcript terms.
6. Otter.ai — Hebrew UI: transcription, speakers, settings.
7. Descript — Hebrew UI: transcript editing, playback.
8. Amberscript — Hebrew UI: transcription service, upload, delivery.

Verified wording: Google „תמלול“, „הגדרות“, „קובץ“, „הורדה“, „עצירה“, „הפעלה“, „חיפוש“ and
„מכשיר“ (the catalog keeps `התקן` for the device, as the glossary records).

Tone: no gendered imperative; action nouns (`שמירה`, `ביטול`, `מחיקה`, `הפעלה`) and the plural
address (`בחרו`, `בדקו`) keep the text neutral for any reader. The speakers are `קול`/`קולות`
(voices); the product's `דובר` stays as evidence only. No idioms or culture-specific examples;
`Speech-to-Text`, file formats, `CPU`, `GPU` and the whisper codes stay untranslated. A
non-gender-neutral or culturally insensitive term is never accepted, even when a compared
product uses it; such a product wording stays in the table as evidence only.

"Compared" gives the product and its Hebrew wording (or the closest feature); "Audit" records the
neutrality and idiom check.
