# German (de) — translation review

Standard (user decision 2026-11-12): every string is compared with a similar app or feature in
the product's own German UI; more popular products rank higher; every string is audited for
gender neutrality and cultural sensitivity. The user approves the German translation; the
mechanical parity check (keys, placeholders, ICU, plural categories) is the standing gate.

## Products compared (popularity, 1 = most popular)

1. Zoom — German UI: meeting/recording/transcript terms.
2. Microsoft Teams — German UI: meeting transcript/recording terms.
3. Google Workspace (Docs/Drive) — German UI: upload, sharing, search, voice typing.
4. Microsoft Word — German UI: dictation/transcription.
5. Apple (macOS/iOS) — German UI: dictation, playback, editing.
6. Otter.ai — German UI: transcription, speakers, settings.
7. Descript — German UI: transcript editing, playback.
8. Amberscript — German UI: transcription service, upload, delivery.

Verified wording: Google „Transkript“, „Gerät“, „Suche“, „pausieren“, „herunterladen“, „beenden“;
Google Drive „Ordner“, „Dateien“, „hochladen“; Apple „umbenennen“, „Ordner“, „Dateien“.

Tone: informal `du` throughout, matching the English UI's direct address and the consumer
products (Google, Apple, Zoom's modern German UI). No generic-masculine terms are used: the
German column has the neutral forms (`Sprechende`, `Anzahl der Sprechenden`, `Max. Sprechende`,
`Trennung der Sprechenden`, `Erkennen der Sprechenden`, `sprechende Person`). The comparison is
for idiomatic wording only: a non-gender-neutral or culturally insensitive term is never
accepted, even when a compared product uses it. A product's GM wording stays in the comparison as
evidence; the audit records the neutral choice (user decision 2026-11-12). No gendered job titles
or culturally loaded idioms are used.

"Compared" gives the product and its German wording; the German column is this catalog's text.
