# Hindi (hi) — translation review

Standard (user decision 2026-11-12): every string is compared with a similar app or feature in
the product's own Hindi UI, for idiomatic wording only; more popular products rank higher. A
non-gender-neutral or culturally insensitive term is never accepted, even when a compared product
uses it: the product's wording stays here as evidence and the catalog uses a neutral alternative.
The mechanical parity check (keys, placeholders, ICU, plural categories) is the standing gate.

## Products compared (popularity, 1 = most popular)

1. Google Workspace (Docs/Drive/Meet) — Hindi UI: upload, search, transcripts.
2. Microsoft (Teams/Word) — Hindi UI: meeting transcripts, connection states, settings.
3. Apple (macOS/iOS) — Hindi UI: dictation, playback, editing.
4. Zoom — Hindi UI: meeting and recording terms.
5. Otter.ai — Hindi UI: transcription and speakers.
6. Descript — Hindi UI: transcript editing, segments.
7. Amberscript — Hindi UI: transcription service.

Hindi imperatives (`करें`, `जाँचें`, `चुनें`) carry no addressee gender, so instructions are neutral
by form. Verbs and adjectives agree with the noun they describe — `कार्य` (m), `फ़ाइल` (f),
`कतार` (f) — never with the reader; the speaker noun is `वक्ता`, masculine in form and used
generically for any speaker. Where a compared product writes `पुनः`, the catalog writes `फिर से`.
Sentences end
with the danda `।`, and Latin words keep a half-width space (`CPU`, `{filename}`). The editor's
shortcut check requires one underlined letter per button, so the buttons show `सहेजें (S)` and
`छोड़ें (D)`.

"Compared" gives the product whose terminology this string reuses; `—` means the string is a
phrase no compared product writes, and only the catalog's own terminology applies.
