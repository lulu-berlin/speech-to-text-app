# French (fr) — translation review

Standard (user decision 2026-11-12): every string is compared with a similar app or feature in
the product's own French UI, for idiomatic wording only; more popular products rank higher. A
non-gender-neutral or culturally insensitive term is never accepted, even when a compared product
uses it: the product's wording stays here as evidence and the catalog uses a neutral alternative.
The user reviews the French wording; the mechanical parity check (keys, placeholders, ICU, plural
categories) is the standing gate.

## Products compared (popularity, 1 = most popular)

1. Zoom — French UI: meeting, recording and transcript terms.
2. Microsoft Teams — French UI: meeting transcript and connection terms.
3. Google Workspace (Docs/Drive) — French UI: import, search, settings.
4. Microsoft Word — French UI: dictation and transcription.
5. Apple (macOS/iOS) — French UI: playback, editing, settings.
6. Otter.ai — French UI: transcription and speakers.
7. Descript — French UI: transcript editing, segments.
8. Amberscript — French UI: transcription service.

Address: `vous` with the imperative (`Vérifiez`, `Déposez`), as the compared French UIs do.
Gender: the products' speaker terms (`intervenant`, `locuteur`) are generic masculine; the
catalog uses the epicene `voix` instead (`Voix`, `Nombre de voix`, `Identification des voix`,
`Renommer la voix 1`) — the app handles the speakers' voices, not the speakers as individuals
(user decision 2026-11-12) — plus `prise de parole` for a turn. No inclusive point (`·`) forms.
Participles agree with the item they describe (a file, a task, a voice), never with a person.
Typography: narrow no-break space before `?`, `;` and `%`, no-break space before `:` and
inside `« »`; typographic apostrophe `’`.

"Compared" gives the product and its French wording; the French column is this catalog's text.
