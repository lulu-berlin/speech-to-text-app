# Italian (it) — translation review

Standard (user decision 2026-11-12): every string is compared with a similar app or feature in
the product's own Italian UI, for idiomatic wording only; more popular products rank higher. A
non-gender-neutral or culturally insensitive term is never accepted, even when a compared product
uses it: the product's wording stays here as evidence and the catalog uses a neutral alternative.
The mechanical parity check (keys, placeholders, ICU, plural categories) is the standing gate.

## Products compared (popularity, 1 = most popular)

1. Google Workspace (Docs/Drive/Meet) — Italian UI: upload, search, transcripts, voice typing.
2. Microsoft (Teams/Word) — Italian UI: meeting transcripts, connection states, settings.
3. Apple (macOS/iOS) — Italian UI: dictation, playback, editing, settings.
4. Zoom — Italian UI: meeting, recording and transcript terms.
5. Otter.ai — Italian UI: transcription and speakers.
6. Descript — Italian UI: transcript editing, segments.
7. Amberscript — Italian UI: transcription service.

Address: `tu` with the imperative (`Controlla`, `Trascina`, `Scegli`), as the compared Italian
consumer UIs write. Gender: the products' speaker nouns (`parlante`, `locutore`) are generic
masculine; the catalog uses the feminine object `voce`/`voci` instead (`Voci`, `Numero di voci`,
`Identificazione delle voci`, `Reimposta il nome della voce 1`), because the app handles the
speakers' voices, not the speakers as people (user decision 2026-11-12). The speaker-mode select
is `Rilevamento delle voci`, so its options (`automatico`, `fisso`, `disattivato`) agree with
`rilevamento`. Status adjectives agree with the noun `attività` (`COMPLETATA`, `FALLITA`,
`ANNULLATA`), never with a person; participles agree with the item they describe. No inclusive
`·` or `*` forms.
Typography: the ellipsis `…`, the em dash `—`, a straight apostrophe `'`, no space before
`?`, `!` or `%`; the access keys are `<u>S</u>alva` and `<u>I</u>gnora`. The transcript outcome
carries its article in the phase string (`{outcome} durante {phase}`, `durante l'identificazione`),
because a template cannot elide. Page names are capitalised as buttons and lowercase in running
text.

"Compared" gives the product and the wording whose terminology this string reuses; `—` means the
string is a phrase no compared product writes, and only the catalog's own terminology applies.
The Italian column is this catalog's text.
