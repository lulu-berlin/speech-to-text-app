# Spanish (Spain) — translation review

Standard (user decision 2026-11-12): every string is compared with a similar app or feature in
the product's own Spanish (Spain) UI, for idiomatic wording only; more popular products rank
higher. A non-gender-neutral or culturally insensitive term is never accepted, even when a
compared product uses it: the product's wording stays here as evidence and the catalog uses a
neutral alternative. The user reviews the Spanish wording; the mechanical checks (parity,
access keys) are the standing gate.

## Products compared (popularity, 1 = most popular)

1. Zoom — Spanish UI: meeting, recording and transcript terms.
2. Microsoft Teams — Spanish UI: meeting transcript and connection terms.
3. Google Workspace (Docs/Drive) — Spanish UI: upload, search, settings.
4. Microsoft Word — Spanish UI: dictation and transcription.
5. Apple (macOS/iOS) — Spanish UI: playback, editing, settings.
6. Otter.ai — Spanish UI: transcription and speakers.
7. Descript — Spanish UI: transcript editing, segments.
8. Amberscript — Spanish UI: transcription service.

Address: `tú` with the imperative (`Comprueba`, `Arrastra`), as consumer apps use.
Gender: the products' speaker term `hablante(s)` is masculine-generic (it takes `el`, `los`,
`nuevo`); the catalog uses `voz`/`voces` instead, because the app handles the speakers' voices,
not the speakers as individuals, and `voz` is feminine so no gender is assumed about a person.
`intervención` (a speaker's turn) is epicene. Participles agree with the file or task, never
with a person (`transcrito`, `completada`). Typography: `¿…?` and `¡…!`, `«»`, a space before
`%`.

"Compared" gives the product and its Spanish wording; the Spanish column is this catalog's text.
