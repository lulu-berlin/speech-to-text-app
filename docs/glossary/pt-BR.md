## Gender and culture

- The speaker noun `falante` is common-gender (the `-e` ending), so no `o/a` or dotted forms are
  needed: `o falante`, `a falante`, plural `falantes`. The compared products' gendered forms
  (`locutor`, `locutora`) stay in the review page as evidence; they are not adopted when a
  neutral alternative exists. The review's comparison is for idiomatic wording only: a
  non-gender-neutral or culturally insensitive term is never accepted, even when a product uses
  it.
- No idioms, no culture-specific examples; the product name `Speech-to-Text` stays untranslated.
- Technical nouns (`threads`, `CPU`, `GPU`, the whisper language codes) stay as the products use
  them.
